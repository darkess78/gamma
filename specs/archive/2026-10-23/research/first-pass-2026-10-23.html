What the public implementation does establish

The official SDK connects games through WebSockets and exposes context and actions. Its integration guidance favors meaningful context over frequent tiny updates, stable action definitions, clear errors, and explicit decision requests when a game is waiting. It warns that she can become distracted or attempt impossible actions.

The same guidance asks integrations to acknowledge a validated action before executing it. That acknowledgment must not be mistaken for verified completion of a real-world task. Speech priorities and final-speech events likewise describe interface behavior, not a general account of attention or consciousness.

The official site presents her as a singer, gamer, and conversational performer. Those are creator descriptions; singing quality and broad game performance were not assessed here.

Public architecture facts and current limits

At checked main e7bf426, the application layer queues jobs, manages context and model operations, calls MCP, and broadcasts job events. The queue loop waits for its current job. Thus an asynchronous implementation is not evidence that the character can continue normal conversation throughout an arbitrary long task. This source was inspected, not executed.

The README identifies Discord, Twitch, and VTube Studio applications. The developer guide also documents shutdown behavior tied to WebSocket clients and private-network deployment limits. Those operational details matter to maintainability, alongside replaceable model adapters. They were not independently tested here.

The June retrospective reports a common repository for the newer services and reuse of integration ideas, but also significant replacement and scope cuts. Public jaison-core source cannot verify which parts of the later ERMA-V deployment are currently active. Her character change should not be treated as a requirement of software evolution, particularly given your wish to keep one continuing Shana.

Evidence limits and audit trail

Research used creator-owned public repositories, release notes, channel inventories, timed transcripts, and selected browser frames. Two Neuro archive leads were third-party uploads of recordings and are labelled accordingly. I did not listen to or continuously watch these videos. Claims about vocal delivery, comic timing, expressions in motion, and perceived responsiveness therefore remain provisional. Automatic transcripts can mishear names, omit words, and merge speakers.

The early Neuro archive transcript was sampled, not read across all nine hours. The official August 2024 and September 2026 videos are edited. The August 31, 2026 unedited archive was reachable and dated by its uploader, but offered no transcript in the browser. It was not used to infer conversational behavior. YouTube's separate transcript service began rejecting requests after earlier retrievals; the normal YouTube transcript panel supplied later samples.

Most video dates above are publication dates. GitHub release and commit dates use UTC. Recording dates and the precise software running at filming time were usually unavailable. Public repository source, release descriptions, and creator demonstrations were kept separate. No project was installed or run. No Gamma source, configuration, documentation, runtime, service, model, or deployment was changed, and no Gamma tests, training, or inference ran.

The research did not rely on the old Gamma Neuro reports as descriptions of private implementations. It is a fresh but selective evidence review, not full coverage of years of streams or private developer discussions.
